This session compiles to a single deck: slides-llms.tex

Built 2026-09-27, as the fourth and final session in the Transformer arc (7: Attention
& Architecture, 8: BERT & T5 variants, 9: this session, 10: Transformers Outside NLP).
Source material is DL4NLP chapters 7 (GPT), 9 (LLMs), and 11 (Training LLMs) -- by far
the largest raw-material surplus of any session built this way (chapter07 ~91 frames,
chapter09 ~100+ real frames across four usable files, chapter11 ~60+ frames), so this
session was scoped aggressively from the start (the "lean from the start" lesson learned
from session 7's overshoot and confirmed by sessions 8 and 10's on-target first builds),
rather than ported close to verbatim and trimmed afterwards.

Sections, in order, with sourcing:

1. Recap: The Decoder-Only Family [original text] -- one frame, closing the taxonomy
   loop that sessions 7/8/10 all deferred to "session 9's subject."

2. GPT-1: The Original Recipe [dl4nlp/chapter07-gpt/slides-71-gpt.tex] -- kept nearly
   complete (5 frames from 6 raw frames): architecture, pretraining objective plus
   BooksCorpus, fine-tuning recipe (the three separate raw frames on task-specific input
   transformations condensed into one), and the parameter-count derivation, kept in the
   same style as BERT's and T5's derivations in session 8 so the three can be compared.
   The "SOTA results" frame was dropped -- its figure (gpt-sota.png) does not exist in
   the source repository's figure/ directory (confirmed by a failed copy, not assumed
   missing), and the result itself is stated in the surrounding text instead.

3. GPT-2: Language Models as Multitask Learners [slides-72-gpt2.tex] -- condensed from
   14 frames to 5: the staged-release "controversy" frame and the architecture frame
   were merged into one text-only "Scale and a New Corpus" frame (its own figures,
   72-gpt2-release.png and 72-gpt2-size.png, were not found in the source figure
   directory either); the two Q&A/factual-knowledge frames were condensed into one,
   keeping the headline 117M-vs-1.5B factual-recall numbers as the session's first
   concrete "scale changes capability" data point, a thread picked up again in the
   scaling-laws section.

4. GPT-3: In-Context Learning at Scale [slides-73-gpt3xshot.tex] -- the biggest single
   condensation in this session: ~20 raw frames (many of them the Schick et al.\
   prompting-history detour, and one near-duplicate figure frame per shot-count) down to
   6. The zero-/one-/few-shot frames (3 raw frames, each showing one figure) were merged
   into a single three-column frame showing all three side by side -- the same kind of
   merge session 8 used for BERT's near-duplicate MLM examples. The Schick (2020)
   "history of prompting" detour (2 frames) was cut in favor of one sentence in the
   "Prompting: Precise Definitions" frame later in this session (Schick is still
   credited there, for verbalizers). Training-corpus and x-shot-comparison figures
   (traincorp.png, xshotlargecorpora.png) were not copied -- their headline numbers
   (499B tokens; few-shot scaling faster than zero-shot) are stated in text instead,
   consistent with the same-repo-image-license reasoning below.

5. GPT-3: Discussion and Limitations [slides-73-gpt3xshot.tex tail; ethics content
   summarized, not sourced from slides-75-discussion.tex -- see note below] -- 2 frames
   (human-vs-machine Turing-style test, bias) plus a bridging frame. Kept deliberately
   short: this is the same kind of "ethics/limitations survey" material session 8 also
   kept brief for BERTology, and this session already has a full skepticism thread later
   (emergent abilities, the scaling-plateau debate) that carries more of the critical
   weight than a long limitations list would.

6. Fine-Tuning and Prompting, Precisely [dl4nlp/chapter09-llm/slides-91-instruction-
   tuning.tex] -- condensed from ~25 real frames (excluding devoured/commented-out ones)
   to 7. Kept: single-task fine-tuning + verbalizers (merged, since the verbalizer figure
   itself, schickverbalizer.png, was not copied), the precise prompting-terminology frame
   (this is the deck's canonical zero-/few-shot definition, referenced back from the
   GPT-3 section rather than duplicated there), multi-task fine-tuning (T0/T5/FLAN),
   T0's held-out-task evaluation protocol, the instruction-tuning terminology frame (with
   an explicit forward-pointer to RLHF in session 11, rather than pulling RLHF-adjacent
   depth into this session), and the fine-tuning conclusions frame (the PaLM 540B
   0.2%-compute-for-9.4%-improvement number). Cut: individual BERT fine-tuning example
   figures, the "continued pretraining" frame (a third, less central sense of
   "fine-tuning," judged not worth a full frame here), and the T0-vs-FLAN comparison and
   FLAN-scaling frames (T0 and FLAN are both already covered qualitatively).

7. Chain-of-Thought Reasoning [chapter09-llm/slides-92-chain-of-thought.tex] --
   condensed from ~25 frames to 6, same pattern as session 8's BERT MLM-example cut: of
   six near-identical worked-example figures (cotex1-6.png), only one (cotex1.png) is
   shown; of two near-identical performance-vs-scale figures, only one
   (cot_performance1.png) is shown. Tree-of-thought (Yao et al., 2023) kept as one frame,
   not derived in depth -- the BeliefBank/creative-writing extensions and the multiple
   sli.do frames were cut as out of scope for this deck. A new closing frame
   ("Is the Chain of Thought Faithful?") was added -- not itself sourced from this file,
   but a deliberate bridge planted here so the DeepSeek section's own unfaithfulness
   finding (below) has somewhere to land structurally, rather than appearing
   unmotivated.

8. Emergent Abilities: A Real Effect, or a Mirage? [chapter09-llm/slides-93-emergent-
   abilities.tex] -- condensed from ~15 frames to 2, keeping only the core claim (Wei et
   al., 2022) and the core rebuttal (Schaeffer et al., 2023, the "mirage of metric
   choice" argument). This is a deliberate scope choice, not a space-filler: the
   metric-choice skepticism is exactly the kind of "is this result real or an artefact"
   check this course's persona is meant to model, and it sets up the scaling-plateau
   debate two sections later. The individual metrics/experiments figures (metrics.png,
   emergent_experiments.png) were cut in favor of stating the argument directly.

9-10-11. Training LLMs: Compute / Memory / Distributed and Efficient
   [chapter11-training-llms/slides-111-compute-memory.tex,
   slides-112-reduce-comp.tex] -- condensed from ~35 real frames (excluding devoured
   ones) to 11. The $C\approx 6PD$ derivation, number representations, and the
   memory-component breakdown are kept close to complete, since this is genuinely new
   material for the course (no prior session covers training cost at all). The INT8
   quantization \textit{algebra} (absmax/zero-point formulas, 3 raw frames) was cut down
   to one descriptive frame with no derivation -- the formulas are a rabbit hole relative
   to this session's scope, and the two Maarten Grootendorst figures illustrating them
   were not copied for the same reason. Data parallelism and tensor parallelism were each
   condensed from 2 raw frames to 1. FlashAttention (4 raw frames) was condensed to 2,
   explicitly framed as a callback to session 7's O(n^2) attention-cost discussion --
   this is one of the strongest concrete callbacks in the whole session, since it
   revisits a cost flagged three sessions ago and finally explains what production
   systems actually do about it.

12. Scaling Laws [chapter11-training-llms/slides-113-scaling.tex] -- condensed from ~30
    real frames (excluding devoured ones) to 7. Kept: Kaplan et al.'s power laws,
    Chinchilla's compute-optimal reframing and headline result (with both of Hoffmann et
    al.'s own figures), the scaling-plateau debate (Sutskever's quote, with an added
    question about incentives -- a deliberate persona-consistent addition, not sourced
    from the slide itself, prompting skepticism about whose interests a "scaling has/
    hasn't plateaued" claim serves), the data-quality/model-size tradeoff with the Phi-4
    example, and a main-takeaway frame. Cut: the optimal-batch-size frames (devoured in
    the source itself), the MMLU/QA-specific Chinchilla figures (the general finding is
    already established by the two figures kept), Epoch AI/Andrew Ng/Jensen Huang
    commentary frames (opinion pieces without a figure worth reproducing), and the
    context-utilization-scaling and "what is a trillion" frames (tangential to this
    session's spine).

13. DeepSeek-R1: Reasoning via Reinforcement Learning [chapter09-llm/slides-94-
    deepseek.tex] -- kept as this session's capstone, condensed from ~20 frames to 8.
    This is genuinely rich, current, real content (not a stub -- see the discovered-gap
    note below), and it ties together three threads planted earlier in this session:
    chain-of-thought (the <think>/<answer> format, and the faithfulness question),
    reinforcement learning (a first, lighter-weight preview of session 11's RLHF, with
    an explicit note that DeepSeek's rewards are automatic/verifiable rather than
    human-preference-based), and the scaling debate (inference-time scaling as a second
    axis for trading compute against quality, argued as directly extending the
    "holistic, not just pretraining" reframing from the scaling-laws section). The
    unfaithfulness-of-reasoning frame is stated in text rather than with its source
    figures (sixplusnine,0.png / sixplusnine,1.png were not copied, to keep this frame
    to a single page) -- the described example (a written trace describing one
    calculation while the final answer matches a different one) is taken directly from
    the source file's own frames, not invented.

14. Closing Synthesis: Four Sessions, One Taxonomy [NEW, original text, 1 frame] -- the
    three-families table one final time, now genuinely complete (GPT/DeepSeek-R1 filled
    into the decoder-only column instead of "session 9's subject"), the payoff frame for
    the whole 7-8-9-10 arc.

Two real bugs were caught and fixed during the build, both found via pdftoppm visual
inspection rather than assumed fine from a clean pdflatex log:
- The auto-generated learning-goals title frame overflowed vertically with the initial
  7-bullet \learninggoals list (visually confirmed: the last bullet was clipped at the
  page edge). Fixed by trimming to 5 bullets and merging the chain-of-thought and
  DeepSeek-R1 goals into one -- the same kind of title-frame overflow session 7 first
  flagged as a recurring risk whenever \learninggoals runs long.
- Two image-heavy frames (the GPU-memory-hierarchy/FlashAttention frame, and the
  DeepSeek example-reasoning-trace frame) triggered "Overfull \vbox" warnings from
  oversized images. Fixed by reducing \includegraphics widths (both confirmed
  visually afterwards to render cleanly, no clipping).
The closing taxonomy table's "NLP" row (originally listing five BERT-family model names
in a narrow column) produced ugly hyphenated line breaks and a genuine overfull \hbox --
fixed by widening the encoder-only column, narrowing decoder-only (its row now has only
two short entries), and replacing the five-model list with "BERT family (session 8)",
consistent with how this table already treats "Beyond NLP" cells.

Build: 102 pages, no errors, no missing figures, no undefined references (confirmed via
log grep for "not found"/"undefined"/"! LaTeX Error"/"Fatal", and by rendering the
title/learning-goals page, the zero/one/few-shot three-column frame, the FlashAttention
memory-hierarchy frame, the DeepSeek reasoning-trace frame, and the closing taxonomy
table with pdftoppm -- all render cleanly with no overflow or clipping). This lands
inside the ~80-110 page target on the first build after fixing the three issues above,
despite this session having by far the largest raw-material surplus of the four
Transformer sessions -- consistent with sessions 8 and 10's "lean from the start"
condensation approach, in contrast to session 7's 156-165-page first-pass overshoot.

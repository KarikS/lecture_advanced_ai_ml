This session compiles to a single deck: slides-transformers-outside-nlp.tex

Built 2026-09-27, as a direct structural fix to a recurring scope concern raised while
building sessions 7-8: with BERT/T5 (session 8) and GPT (session 9) both purely NLP, the
whole four-session Transformer arc read as "general Transformer course, NLP only," and
the light CV/multimodal callback frames added to sessions 7-8 only partially addressed
it. This session is the real fix -- a full session dedicated to non-NLP Transformer
applications.

Unlike sessions 7-8, there is no i2dl or DL4NLP source material for this topic (neither
course covers vision/multimodal Transformers), so nothing here is "adapted from this
course's usual sources" in the way earlier sessions were. Content follows two external,
license-checked sources instead (see below), and is freshly written throughout -- there
is no _sources/ directory for this session, since nothing was copied from an existing
Beamer deck.

Sections, in order, with sourcing:

1. Recap and Roadmap [original text] -- one frame bridging from session 8's brief
   CV/multimodal callbacks to this session's depth.

2. Vision Transformers, In Depth [Hugging Face Community Computer Vision Course, MIT
   license, verified directly from the repo's LICENSE file] -- patch embedding mechanics
   (own knowledge of the well-established Dosovitskiy et al. 2020 architecture, not
   quoted from a source), and the CNN-vs-ViT inductive-bias argument (locality,
   translational equivariance, the data-size crossover point), which IS drawn directly
   from the course's "Vision Transformers for Image Classification" chapter and its
   transfer-learning framing.

3. DETR: A Third Encoder-Decoder Pattern [same HF course, "DETR" chapter] -- the
   deliberate conceptual hook for this section: every encoder-decoder example so far
   (T5, Whisper) decodes autoregressively; DETR's parallel, non-autoregressive decoding
   via learned object queries and bipartite matching is a genuinely different pattern
   within the same family, not just another model. The minimal PyTorch implementation
   shown is the HF course's own code (itself adapted from Carion et al.'s original
   minimal implementation) -- included specifically to make the "no anchors, no NMS"
   simplicity claim concrete rather than asserted.

4. Combining Modalities: Two Architectural Patterns [LMU Multimodal Deep Learning
   seminar, https://slds-lmu.github.io/seminar_multimodal_dl/, licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0
   -- confirmed directly in the book's own front matter, not assumed from being in the
   same GitHub org as i2dl/DL4NLP, which are CC BY 4.0 and NOT the same license] --
   contrastive dual-encoders (CLIP) vs. fusion architectures (UniT), framed as a genuine
   design choice (fast retrieval/zero-shot vs. fine-grained cross-modal interaction), not
   just two examples. Image captioning and text-to-image generation are kept deliberately
   short: captioning is explicitly named as "the same encoder-decoder pattern as Whisper,
   nothing new," and text-to-image is framed as a callback to session 6's diffusion
   models plus session 7's cross-attention derivation, specifically to avoid re-teaching
   material that already exists elsewhere in this course.

5. Unified, Domain-Agnostic Architectures [same LMU seminar, "Multi-Purpose Models"
   chapter] -- Gato kept as the one fully-explained example (its "tokenize everything,
   including robot actions, then it's just next-token prediction" pitch is the cleanest
   possible statement of this session's overall thesis); UniT, OFA, and LIMoE mentioned
   at landscape level only, not derived in depth -- the seminar source covers six such
   architectures (MultiModel, UniT, OFA, Gato, LIMoE, muNet) and going deep on all of them
   would have re-created session 7/8's overshoot problem for comparatively little
   additional insight, since they mostly vary in tokenization/routing details rather than
   introducing new attention mechanics.

6. Video: One More Dimension [original text, high-level only] -- deliberately brief (one
   frame): space-time patch tokenization and the factorized-attention response to the
   resulting O(n^2) blowup, framed entirely as a callback to session 7's efficiency
   discussion rather than new content. Video Transformers are a large enough topic to
   justify their own session; going deeper here was judged out of scope for a single
   session already covering vision, detection, and multimodal architectures.

7. Closing Synthesis: Four Sessions, One Architecture [original content] -- the
   three-families table one final time, now fully populated across NLP, vision, audio,
   and cross-modal/unified entries -- the payoff frame for the whole 7-8-9-10 arc.

Not included: Stanford CS25 ("Transformers United") was checked for scope/topic-list
purposes only and confirmed to carry no license or reuse statement anywhere (Stanford
copyright by default) -- no content, code, or figures from it appear anywhere in this
deck, per the license concern raised when this session was first proposed.

Figures: no external images were downloaded or embedded from either source course --
both courses' own figures were left out entirely because their individual provenance
wasn't independently verified (some images in course material like this often originate
from the papers being described, under their own separate copyright, not from the
course's own MIT/CC license). Instead, three original TikZ diagrams were drawn from
scratch (patch-embedding illustration, DETR architecture, CLIP dual-encoder diagram),
plus one compiled standalone image (figure/vit-patches.png, the title-page figure,
rendered from the same patch-embedding TikZ code via a separate pdflatex+pdftoppm pass
since \titlefigure requires an image file, not inline TikZ).

Two real bugs were caught and fixed during the build: the DETR and CLIP TikZ diagrams
both used fixed absolute coordinates with manually-guessed minimum-width boxes, which
were too narrow for their actual text ("Transformer encoder", "text encoder
(Transformer)") and caused overlapping boxes and garbled overlapping text. Fixed by
switching both diagrams to the tikz `positioning` library's relative placement
(`right=of`, `below=of`) with `align=center` and no manual width, so box size follows
content instead of a guessed constant.

First build: 41 pages, no errors. Flagged as well under the ~90-100 page estimate,
deliberately (depth-over-breadth scoping), for the user's review.

Second pass, same day, per the user's explicit request ("add material back aiming for
similar scope [to] the others"): added six new frames/subsections rather than padding
existing ones --

- Efficient Vision Transformers (Swin Transformer, DeiT) -- inserted between the
  inductive-bias frame and DETR. Swin is a direct callback to session 7's local-attention
  efficiency discussion; DeiT directly answers the "needs massive data" pain point raised
  two frames earlier, and is a vision-domain callback to session 8's DistilBERT.
- Segmentation Transformers (MaskFormer) -- a new section after DETR, completing the
  classification/detection/segmentation trio of core vision tasks, plus a synthesis table
  comparing ViT/DETR/MaskFormer's query mechanisms directly.
- CLIP's actual training objective (the N x N contrastive loss, zero-shot classification
  mechanics) -- the original build only described CLIP qualitatively; this adds the actual
  mechanism.
- OFA promoted from a one-bullet mention to its own frame, since it's conceptually
  distinct from UniT (task-in-the-input seq2seq vs. task-specific decoder) and directly
  extends session 8's T5 text-to-text framing into a cross-modal setting.
- Video expanded from 1 frame to 4 (the O(n^2) problem stated on its own; ViViT's four
  factorization variants; tubelet vs. uniform-sampling tokenization; TimeSformer's divided
  space-time attention) -- sourced from the HF course's actual Unit 7 content, not just
  asserted at a high level as in the first pass.
- Text-to-image and image captioning expanded using material already gathered from the
  LMU seminar but not used in the first pass: the Meshed-Memory Transformer's specific
  innovations for captioning, and the real GAN-to-DALL-E-to-diffusion evolution
  (StackGAN/AttnGAN, DALL-E 1, GLIDE, DALL-E 2/unCLIP, Imagen, Parti, Stable Diffusion)
  for generation, instead of naming only DALL-E 2/Imagen/Stable Diffusion in passing.

One real bug caught in this pass: none -- the two TikZ diagrams fixed in the first pass
were not touched again, and the new tables (CV-tasks synthesis) were built with the same
fixed-width `p{}` column pattern already proven not to overflow in sessions 7-8 and this
session's closing-synthesis table, checked again by rendering with pdftoppm rather than
assumed safe.

Second build (this pass): 66 pages, no errors.

Third pass, same day, per explicit instruction: no new topics, only go deeper on
subtopics already present, with an intuitive/high-level framing to match this course's
established style, and add visualizations wherever they help. Nine additions, all
worked examples or analogies for existing subtopics, not new material:

- ViT patch tokenization: a concrete worked example with real numbers (224x224 image,
  16x16 patches -> 196 patches + [CLS] = 197 tokens; why patches instead of per-pixel
  tokens, tied back to session 7's O(n^2) cost).
- CNN vs. ViT inductive bias: a concrete "shifted photo" thought experiment (a cat
  centered vs. in a corner) making the locality/translation-equivariance argument
  tangible instead of abstract.
- Swin's shifted windows: a "reading a book paragraph by paragraph, then shifting where
  paragraphs begin" analogy, explicitly tied back to Transformer-XL's segment recurrence
  (session 7) as the same underlying idea (cross a hard boundary without full quadratic
  attention).
- DETR bipartite matching: a worked numeric example (3 queries, 2 ground-truth objects,
  a small matching diagram) -- the same "worked example" convention session 7 used for
  BPE. This diagram needed three rounds of fixing: edge labels initially collided at the
  lines' crossing point, then collided with a ground-truth box after the first fix;
  final version places both labels directly on their own line segment, well before the
  crossing point, confirmed by rendering at 100dpi.
- A new similarity-matrix diagram and "matching game in a room" analogy for CLIP's
  contrastive loss -- the column labels (image1..5) initially overlapped illegibly at
  scriptsize/scale=0.62; fixed by rotating them 90 degrees (vertical text takes far less
  horizontal room than horizontal text at tight column spacing) and enlarging the grid.
- A concrete task-based comparison (reverse image search vs. visual question answering)
  for when dual-encoder vs. fusion architectures actually differ in practice.
- A concrete token-sequence diagram for Gato, showing what one training example
  literally looks like (image patch tokens | text tokens | action tokens) -- the three
  captions initially overlapped since two were single-line and wide; fixed by making all
  three two-line and staggering the middle one vertically.
- A "how you actually watch a movie" analogy plus a joint-vs-factorised attention
  diagram for ViViT -- captions under each panel initially overlapped since neither had
  a bounded text width; fixed with explicit `text width` and by widening the gap between
  the two panels.

Every new diagram was rendered and visually inspected after fixing, not just after
first writing -- four of the five new diagrams needed at least one overlap fix, all
caught this way rather than left in the file unverified.

Final build: 84 pages (up from 41 before this session's second and third passes), no
errors, no missing figures, no undefined references. All frames added in this pass are
